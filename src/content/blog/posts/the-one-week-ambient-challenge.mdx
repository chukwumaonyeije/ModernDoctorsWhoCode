Nine lessons in, you have a workflow, a review discipline, and a vendor-neutral framework. The only thing left to find out is whether any of it actually changes your week.

Clinical implementation should happen only with an appropriately approved system, and according to your practice's, institution's, and applicable privacy, consent, security, and legal requirements. This is not a lab exercise with a fictional patient. This is a real audit of real practice.

## The Challenge

For seven consecutive days, observe your ambient workflow deliberately instead of just using it. At the end of each clinical session, record a handful of things while they are still fresh: approximate documentation time, editing time, the errors that came up, what got omitted, whether you spent less time interacting with the computer, whether you felt more attentive to the patient, whether the generated note actually represented your reasoning, and what you changed in your workflow as a result.

A simple daily table works better than trying to remember at the end of the week:

| Question | Notes |
|---|---|
| How much documentation time did I save or add? | |
| How much editing was required? | |
| What error occurred most often? | |
| What information was commonly omitted? | |
| Did I look at the patient more? | |
| Did I feel less cognitively fragmented? | |
| Did the note capture my reasoning? | |
| What will I change tomorrow? | |

## Three Capstone Questions

**Did I listen more?** Did the technology change how much uninterrupted narrative you allowed before redirecting the conversation.

**Did I think more?** Did reducing manual documentation create actual room for clinical reasoning, or did it just get filled with something else.

**Did patient care feel different?** More human, more efficient, or simply different in a way you had not anticipated.

<Callout type="clinical">
The success of ambient AI should not be measured solely by words per minute or minutes saved. The more meaningful question is whether the technology helps you devote more attention to the work that requires a physician.
</Callout>

## Look Beyond Documentation Time

Ambient documentation deserves evaluation as a clinical practice intervention, not only a productivity tool. Beyond the daily table, ask yourself honestly at the end of the week whether you interrupted less and looked at the patient more, whether you had more mental space to build a differential and noticed things you might otherwise have missed, whether the final notes were better or simply longer, and whether the encounter itself felt more conversational or, at any point, more intrusive.

Finish the week by completing one sentence honestly: ambient documentation is valuable to my practice when it ____________.

Answer that sentence specifically, in your own words, before you decide whether to continue, modify, change platforms, or stop.
